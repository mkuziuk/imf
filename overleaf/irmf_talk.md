# IRMF talk

A Beamer deck (21 main slides plus 4 backup) introducing intrinsic robust
multiscale filtering for an audience that does not yet know IMF or EMD. The PDF
has 32 pages because slide 3 is a seven-step overlay animation. Slide numbers in
the footer count frames, not pages.

## Compile

From `overleaf/`, with a TeX installation that includes Beamer:

```bash
latexmk -pdf irmf_talk.tex
```

In Overleaf, select `irmf_talk.tex` as the main document. All figures are in
`figures/irmf-talk/`, except `figures/real-vs-calculated-limits/baseline_degradation_mean_scaled_rmse.png`,
which is reused unchanged.

## Structure

1. Problem: signal + noise + contamination.
2. IMF in pictures: a window slides along the series (animation).
3. IMF recursion with the linear and smooth-absolute contrasts.
4. Components without and with 20% contamination: real vs linear IMF vs IRMF.
5. Error per component, without and with contamination.
6. Additive sweep: baseline-relative degradation.
7. Second model: contamination replaces the signal.
8. Both-models error map and sweep slices.
9. Why larger replacing contamination can help IRMF.
10. EMD sifting, the two audio datasets, and classification results.
11. `pimf` usage.
12. Why it works: notation and the boxed expansion, a picture of fit vs target,
    Proposition 4.1, and later components (work in progress).
13. Summary. Backup: 100-repeat sweep slice, stage-1 bias and spread,
    implementation details.

## Notes on the claims

- With 100 repeats, the masked-model IRMF mean *scaled* RMSE falls from 0.0093 at
  2 sigma to 0.0082 from 6 sigma on, about 12%. The *unscaled* mean over nine
  stages is flat, about 0.018; the improvement sits in stage 1. The older deck's
  3-repeat statement that robust mean RMSE "falls 13% from 2 sigma to 8 sigma"
  refers to the unscaled mean and is not supported at 100 repeats.
- The theory slides state Proposition 4.1 as in `proposition_4_1_lemma_4_2.tex`.
  They do not compare the fit with the clean signal. Results for the second and
  later IRMF components are presented as work in progress.
