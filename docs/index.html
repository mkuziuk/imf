Multiscale signal decomposition

Filter coarse to fine.
Robust at every scale.

A contaminated signal is decomposed into multiscale components by recursively fitting a local model at shrinking bandwidths. The quadratic contrast yields the linear filter in closed form; the smoothed absolute contrast, minimized by gradient descent with a bounded score, yields its robust counterpart. Every curve below is recomputed live by the project's NumPy implementation running in your browser.

\( Y^{(k+1)} = Y^{(k)} - \widetilde{S}^{(k)} \) \( \widetilde{S}^{(k)}_t = \arg\min_x \textstyle\sum_u K_{h_k}(t-u)\, \rho\big(Y^{(k)}_u - x\big) \)

01 · Observation

Signal under contamination

Gaussian noise \( \varepsilon_i \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma^2) \) plus signed exponential contamination \( C_i \sim \pm\,\mathrm{Exp}(b) \) applied where the Bernoulli mask \( M_i \sim \mathrm{Bern}(p) \) fires. Marked points are the contaminated indices.

02 · Decomposition

Stage-by-stage components

Left: the closed-form linear filter. Right: gradient descent with the selected contrast. The dashed reference in each panel is the same filter applied to the clean signal \( X \) — the component the method would extract with no noise at all. Both columns share the y-scale per stage.

03 · Reconstruction

Exact reconstruction, denoised estimate

The decomposition is exact by construction: \( Y = \sum_k \widetilde S_k + R_{K+1} \) holds to machine precision for both methods. The denoised estimate \( \widehat X = \sum_k \widetilde S_k \) discards only the final residual.

04 · Gradient descent

Convergence at every scale

Per-stage trace of \( \max_t |x^{(m+1)}_t - x^{(m)}_t| \) for the vectorized update \( x^{(m+1)} = x^{(m)} + \eta \sum_u w_{t,u}\, \psi\big(r_u - x^{(m)}\big) \) with \( \psi_H(r) = \mathrm{erf}\big(\tfrac{r}{\sqrt2 H}\big) \) bounded in \([-1, 1]\) — the boundedness of the score is exactly what buys robustness to the contamination.

05 · Stage errors

Real vs calculated components

\( \mathrm{RMSE}_k = \lVert \widetilde S_k(Y) - \widetilde S_k(X) \rVert_n \): the distance between the component computed from the observation and the same component computed from the clean signal. The \( a^{k/2} \) normalization asks whether the error grows exactly with the bandwidth schedule — flat lines mean it does.
